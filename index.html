AI & LLMs

Anthropic and OpenAI staff openly question whether their top models are safe to release

Axios reported on 24 November that employees at both labs have voiced doubts that certain unreleased frontier models can be launched safely — this after OpenAI earlier slowed its “Astra” model because it couldn’t rule out that it had hit the “critical” threshold in its preparedness framework. Anthropic’s counter-argument: keeping a capable model internal doesn’t actually make it safe. (a recurring 2026 theme — over 1,200 AI-company staff have signed a “Pacing the Frontier” petition this year; this is reporting on internal sentiment, not a formal policy change)

Unite.AI (via Axios)

Google ships Gemini 4 Neon — a cheaper coding model it says matches Opus 5.5 and GPT-6.2 Sol

Announced 18 November, Gemini 4 Neon is a mid-tier model aimed at coding and agentic work, priced at $3 per million input / $15 per million output tokens. Google claims state-of-the-art secure-code results on the hard tier of AutoBaxBench (76%) and ~32% lower cost per task than Gemini 4 Carbon in third-party evals. It replaces Argon in the Gemini app picker; Argon’s introductory pricing ends 1 December. (benchmark figures are Google’s own or early third-party reporting; available via AI Studio, the Gemini API, and Antigravity for paid tiers)

Google (blog.google)

OpenAI begins testing “Dots” — persistent, always-on agents that run between chats

OpenAI is reported to be trialling “Dots,” agents that keep running in the background between conversations, with a small set of ChatGPT Plus users and some Business workspaces. Separately, OpenAI says its agents now complete about 46% of research tasks estimated at 2–4 hours of human work, up from 23% in March. (limited test; sourced from aggregated weekly agent-news reporting and OpenAI’s own eval figures rather than a primary launch post — treat as early/unconfirmed)

Automotive & MBD

Pony.ai and Toyota put a fully driverless bZ4X robotaxi into paid service in Guangzhou

The two companies launched commercial, fully driverless rides with the mass-produced bZ4X — Toyota’s first series-production battery-electric robotaxi, built on Pony.ai’s Gen-7 autonomous system — starting in Guangzhou’s Nansha district, bookable 24/7 via the PonyPilot app. They are targeting roughly 1,000 vehicles across major Chinese cities as production scales. (announced 15–16 November; a mass-produced OEM robotaxi, not a prototype — the industrialisation step most AV programmes are chasing)

CnEVPost

New SAE paper: AI-driven predictive fault management for ADAS controllers under ISO 26262

A 2026 SAE Technical Paper proposes a framework that combines AI-based predictive fault models with ISO 26262 safety mechanisms in ADAS controllers — shifting from reactive diagnostics toward anticipating faults before they violate a safety goal. Directly in the AI-in-functional-safety lane relevant to MBD work. (research paper, not a product release; paywalled on SAE MOBILUS — abstract is open)

SAE MOBILUS

Quiet week for OEM/AUTOSAR newsflow — demand signal in the camera market

Hard news on Classic/Adaptive AUTOSAR stacks and OEM functional-safety was thin over the last 48 hours. The notable data point: a 24 November market study forecasts the automotive-camera market growing at a 7.9% CAGR to ~$15.82 billion by 2033, crediting ADAS and AI perception as the main drivers. (market-research release, not company news — included as a sector signal, treat the forecast numbers with the usual caution)

India · Cars & Bikes

Toyota Innova Hycross facelift due to launch today — likely India’s first flex-fuel strong hybrid

Toyota has teased the facelifted Innova Hycross for a 25 November launch, with the headline change widely expected to be a flex-fuel version of its 2.0-litre petrol strong-hybrid powertrain, able to run on high ethanol blends (up to E85/E100). Estimated pricing runs roughly ₹20–35 lakh (ex-showroom). (launch date is from Toyota’s teaser and widely reported, but a couple of outlets list the 26th; the flex-fuel hybrid spec and final price were not officially confirmed at the time of writing — a genuine powertrain-engineering first for India if it lands)

Autocar India

Royal Enfield Himalayan 750 heads to India — pricing expected at Motoverse this week

Royal Enfield unveiled the Himalayan 750 at EICMA with an all-new 743cc parallel-twin (63 Nm, five ride modes, cruise control, TPMS), a big step up from the 450. Its India debut, pricing and bookings are expected at Motoverse (HillTop, Goa, 27–29 November), alongside the Continental GT 750 and an electric Flying Flea S6. Estimated ~₹4.0–4.8 lakh. (bike is officially revealed; the India price and booking timing are expectations until Royal Enfield confirms them at the event)
