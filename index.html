AI & LLMs

Anthropic IPO: valuation reportedly trimmed to ~$1.5–1.6T as the mid-November window nears

Anthropic filed its S-1 publicly on 26 October (Nasdaq, ticker “ANT”) and is targeting a listing as early as the week of 16 November. Reporting now says institutional investors are pushing the valuation down to roughly $1.5–1.6 trillion from a ~$2 trillion target amid a tech sell-off; the prospectus details hundreds of billions in multi-year compute commitments among the key risks. Nothing is priced until the roadshow closes and the SEC declares the registration effective. (valuation figures reported, not official)

Bloomberg

OpenAI’s GPT-6 family goes live inside Google Cloud’s model garden

Google Cloud is making OpenAI models — GPT-6 Astra, GPT-6.1 Sol and GPT-6 Luna — available to enterprise customers, rolling out now in private preview via Vertex AI. It is a notable cross-vendor move: two direct rivals meeting on the same managed platform, with customers able to call OpenAI alongside Gemini. (official — Google Cloud blog)

Google Cloud

Google cuts Gemini video-generation prices ~30% with new Omni 1.2 Flash

Coverage dated 5 November reports Google’s Gemini Omni 1.2 Flash generates native ~15-second clips with better consistency while cutting prices about 30%, accessible through Google AI Studio and Vertex AI. The price-cutting underlines how fast inference costs for generative video are falling as the model race intensifies. (reported — not yet confirmed against Google’s own post)

Automotive & MBD

VW launches full-scenario Level 2++ ADAS and leans hard on its GAIA world model

Volkswagen rolled out a full-scenario Level 2++ ADAS within its VEGA technology stack in China, validated with its in-house GAIA 2.0 world model — which VW says runs simulations equivalent to ~5 million km of driving. The company targets L3 technology readiness in 2027 and has secured an L3 road-test permit in Hefei. The theme worth noting for MBD teams: simulation/world-models as the primary validation path for ML-in-the-loop functions. (OEM announcement)

Autonomous Vehicle International

Baidu unveils a closed-loop simulator generating up to 100,000 km of driving a day

Reports dated 3–5 November say Baidu Apollo showed a closed-loop simulation model that can generate up to ~100,000 km of synthetic driving per day, pitched as a way to stress whole trips before deploying less-predictable end-to-end stacks at scale. Same lesson as VW: the verification problem for AI-driven vehicles is being met with massive simulated mileage, not just road testing. (reported)

WTVB / Reuters

NHTSA proposes an optional cyan “ADS marker lamp” for driverless vehicles

A proposed rule published 26 October would add an optional cyan marker lamp signalling that a vehicle is operating driverless; NHTSA said it “lacks sufficient information” to require it on every ADS vehicle. Public comments are due 25 November. A small but concrete step in the regulatory scaffolding around autonomy. (proposed rule — comment period open)
