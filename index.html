AI & LLMs

Anthropic launches Claude Ship — AI agents that review, fix and approve pull requests

On 23 November Anthropic released Claude Ship, a set of GitHub-focused agents: Code Review (now generally available) plus two betas — PR Steward, which triages and fixes pull requests, and Claude Approvals, which can approve code changes. It is rolling out to Claude Team and Enterprise plans, and Anthropic recommends running the approval agent in observe-only mode first. (landed days after Anthropic’s record IPO; agent-driven code approval is opt-in and GitHub-only at launch)

TechCrunch

Anthropic prices the biggest IPO ever — ~$81B raised, stock closes first day +17%

Anthropic priced its IPO at $83/share and raised about $81.3 billion (up to ~$93.5 billion if underwriters exercise a 30-day option), the largest IPO on record. The stock (Nasdaq: ANT) opened on 19 Nov at $111.50 (+34%) and closed its first day at $97.17, up ~17%. A separate ~$26 billion private placement from Google, Microsoft, NVIDIA and others lifts total fundraising to roughly $107 billion. (figures from offering coverage; the over-allotment option was unexercised at first close, and some outlets cited a ~$2T market cap intraday)

Al Jazeera

Google ships Gemini 4 Hydrogen — a faster, cheaper model for coding and agents

Google announced Gemini 4 Hydrogen on 12 November, a lower-cost, lower-latency member of the Gemini 4 family aimed at everyday coding and agentic work, and the model behind its new Antigravity IDE. Free Gemini-app users reach it via automatic model selection; developers get it through AI Studio and the Gemini API with a limited free tier. (pricing and benchmark figures in circulation are largely from third-party blogs, not Google’s own pages)

Third-party model trackers list OpenAI releasing GPT-6.2 around 17 November in two tiers — Astra (reported ~$8/$40 per million tokens) and the cheaper Sol (~$2/$10) — positioned against Claude Opus 5.5 and Gemini 4. (tracker-sourced and not confirmed against an OpenAI announcement; treat the names, date and pricing as unverified)

Automotive & MBD

Waymo pauses freeway robotaxi rides across the US after a California DMV order

After a 5 November incident in which three Waymo vehicles drove into a Bay Area construction zone (and a 6 November clip of one passing police cars with lights flashing), California’s DMV barred Waymo from state highways from 11 November until it demonstrates corrective action. Waymo then voluntarily paused freeway operations in its other markets — Phoenix, Miami, Austin and LA — while surface-street service continues. (a regulator forcing an operating-domain rollback — exactly the validation-and-corrective-action loop ISO 21448/SOTIF work exists to pre-empt)

Al Jazeera

LG earns the first ASIL D product certification for an automotive central gateway

TÜV Rheinland certified LG’s Central Communication Unit (CCU 2.0) — the central gateway built for the Genesis GV90 — to ISO 26262 ASIL D, which LG says is the first ASIL D product certification in this category (it earned an ASIL B camera-module cert back in 2021). The gateway is the hub that links ECUs and manages in-vehicle and external data. (announced ~8–9 Nov; a product-level cert at the highest integrity class, not just a process cert)

Korea Herald

STMicroelectronics unveils a unified STM32 platform spanning automotive and “physical AI”

At its STM32 Summit (3–4 Nov) ST announced a single MCU platform across industrial, automotive and robotics, reusing architecture, IP and the STM32Cube software stack. The automotive STM32A family scales from entry nodes up to ASIL D-capable zonal endpoints; ST also added a “STM32Cube for Agentic AI” framework and Edge AI tools, with VW Group endorsing the family. (mostly ST’s own materials; shipping dates for the STM32A parts were not given)

India · Cars & Bikes

Toyota Innova Hycross facelift launches tomorrow — likely India’s first flex-fuel strong hybrid

Toyota has teased the facelifted Innova Hycross ahead of a 25 November launch, with updated styling, larger screens and — widely expected — India’s first flex-fuel strong hybrid: a 2.0-litre petrol-electric hybrid able to run on blends up to E85 ethanol. Price is expected around ₹20–35 lakh (ex-showroom). (launch date from Toyota’s teaser; the flex-fuel hybrid and the exact ethanol blend are expected, not yet officially confirmed — a notable powertrain-engineering first if it lands)

Autocar India

Honda launches a made-in-India Rebel 500 at ₹4.50 lakh

Honda relaunched the Rebel 500 cruiser on 19 November at ₹4.50 lakh (Standard) and ₹4.90 lakh (E-Clutch), ex-showroom Bengaluru — about ₹62,000 cheaper than the 2025 CBU version, thanks to ~50% local assembly (the 471 cc, 46 bhp parallel-twin engine is still imported). Bookings are open. (local assembly, not full local manufacture; ex-showroom prices, so on-road costs run higher)

Autocar India

VinFast’s pint-sized VF3 EV nears an India launch

VinFast’s VF3 — a sub-4-metre micro-EV first shown at Auto Expo 2025 and expected to be heavily localised — is listed for an imminent India launch at an estimated ₹7.5–10 lakh, pitched against the MG Comet and Tata Tiago EV. (rumour on timing: listings say “tentative November” but VinFast India has not officially confirmed a date, and the price is an estimate)
