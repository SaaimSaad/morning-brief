AI & LLMs

Anthropic expands “AI for Science” and reports drug-discovery results

On 28 October Anthropic said it is backing 20 scientific institutions and research groups under an enlarged AI for Science program; the day before it reported early results from research partnerships with six drug developers. Among them, reporting says Claude-built agents at Generate Biomedicines produced around 35,000 antibody designs in roughly a month for a potential food-allergy treatment — results not yet validated in animals or humans, so treat them as design throughput, not proven therapeutics.

anthropic.com

Anthropic widens US government deal: 40% off Claude, extended to states and cities

Anthropic expanded its General Services Administration agreement (announced 27 October), giving federal buyers a 40% discount on Claude Enterprise, Claude for Government and the Claude API, and for the first time extending that pricing to state, local, tribal and territorial governments. Anthropic estimates the arrangement could save governments on the order of $1bn.

OpenAI’s “GPT-6.1 Terra” surfaces in Microsoft config — still not released (rumour / leak)

A merged Microsoft pull request (16 October) pointed evaluation configs at a “gpt-6.1-terra” judge model, reviving talk of a cheaper, stronger Terra model said to beat GPT-6.1 Sol. As of now there is no OpenAI launch date and it is absent from the official model catalogue; one report warns a launch could slip into November. Unconfirmed until OpenAI announces it.

Amazon eases its internal push for AI use

Coverage on 28 October reports Amazon dialling back pressure on staff to adopt AI tools, a counterpoint to the prevailing “mandate it everywhere” narrative, and lands alongside continued scrutiny of Anthropic’s IPO-filing disclosures. A useful reminder that enterprise AI adoption is proving messier than the launch cadence suggests.

autosar.org

NHTSA’s Cybercab fight is becoming a homologation case study

The live regulatory thread for anyone in functional safety: NHTSA has ordered Tesla to defend, under oath, how it self-certified the steering-wheel-free Cybercab against specific standards (FMVSS 101, 102, 108, 111, 126) without holding a Part 555 exemption. It crystallises the gap between an autonomy stack and the homologation/FMVSS case a driverless vehicle must still clear to be sold.
