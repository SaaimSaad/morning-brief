AI & LLMs

AI sell-off drags Anthropic’s IPO valuation down to ~$1.5–1.6T

Per WSJ (3 Nov) and Reuters reporting, institutional investors who attended Anthropic’s San Francisco investor day are now signalling a listing value of roughly $1.5–1.6 trillion — below the ~$2T discussed in recent months, though still above its last private mark of $1.41T. They cited choppy markets, AI-overexposure fears, political pushback on data centres and uncertainty over when hyperscaler AI spend turns to profit. Anthropic is expected to propose a price range (a traditional roadshow), launch it as soon as this week, and debut on Nasdaq (ticker “ANT”) the week before Thanksgiving. (reported via WSJ/Reuters; valuation and dates not yet official)

Automotive & MBD

Tesla’s Texas robotaxi fleet closes on Waymo; unsupervised FSD slips to Q2 2027

Texas registry data this week put Tesla’s registered robotaxi fleet at roughly 1,200–1,250 vehicles — within about 15–55 of Waymo’s registered count — though registrations don’t show how many actually carry paying riders. Separately, Tesla has pushed unsupervised FSD for customer cars to Q2 2027, and only by enrolling owners’ vehicles into its geofenced robotaxi fleet, not as a free-roaming feature. For MBD/safety engineers the signal is that the hard validation gate — removing the human fallback — keeps sliding right. (fleet figures reported, vary by snapshot; FSD timeline per Tesla)

Tesla North

The OEM compute war: Qualcomm now BMW’s lead silicon supplier, Nvidia and Mobileye chasing design wins

The structural story worth tracking for an OEM move: Qualcomm has become BMW Group’s lead compute-silicon provider for next-gen digital cockpit, ADAS and automated driving (announced July 2026), while Nvidia and Mobileye race to lock in automakers for their own chip-plus-software AD platforms. This consolidation around a few central SoCs is what pulls distributed Classic-AUTOSAR ECU work toward centralised, Adaptive/SDV software stacks — exactly the shift an MBD engineer moving to an OEM is positioning for. (ongoing/structural, not breaking this week; BMW–Qualcomm deal dated July 2026)

BMW Blog

EV demand slowdown still forcing OEM cuts in the US

The post-incentive EV cooldown continues to reshape legacy-OEM strategy: GM has run multiple 2026 layoff waves tied to slowing EV demand (thousands of jobs across Michigan, Ohio and Tennessee, plus a ~$1.6B EV-related charge), and smaller players such as Lucid have cut ~18% of staff. The read-through: OEM hiring and programme priorities are tilting back toward profitable ICE/hybrid lines and software, not volume EVs. (trend through 2026; some underlying figures date to late 2025/early 2026 — directional, not a single-day event)
