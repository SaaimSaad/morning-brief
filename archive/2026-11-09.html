Google DeepMind shows AI agents flagging weaknesses in 1024-bit RSA

On 6 Nov, DeepMind reportedly presented research in which AI agents, guided by researchers, helped identify two weaknesses in 1024-bit RSA cryptographic systems — a concrete example of frontier models being pointed at hard security and number-theory problems rather than chat. (reported via weekly roundups; worth checking against DeepMind’s own writeup before relying on it)

AI News roundup (8 Nov)

OpenAI runs a multi-agent pipeline against the Linux kernel

OpenAI described a staged multi-agent system (Scout, Analyst, Triage, ExploitDev and patch agents) run on a mid-October Linux master branch: 2,308 candidate findings, 1,724 vulnerabilities reproduced in instrumented VMs, and 948 distinct local-privilege-escalation exploits built. It is pitched alongside OpenAI moving its Preparedness Framework to “Critical” with a dedicated security model. (reported via AI digests; verify specifics against OpenAI’s own publication)

AI Daily Digest (6 Nov)

Android Studio opens up to 40+ coding agents, including Claude and Codex

Google’s Android Studio now lets developers switch among 40+ coding agents — Claude Agent, OpenAI Codex and others — via the open Agent Client Protocol, with new CLI plugins for Claude Code and Codex. Connected agents get access to project info, build systems, the emulator and Logcat. A small but real sign of agent tooling standardising around a shared protocol. (reported; feature-level, not a model release)

Automotive & MBD

VW Group China lands a Level 3 road-test permit in Hefei, eyes 2027 readiness

Volkswagen Group China said on 26 Oct it secured a Level 3 automated-driving road-test permit in Hefei and has begun real-world testing and data collection, under what it calls the toughest L3 framework yet. The work runs through CARIZON, its JV with Horizon Robotics; VW targets L3 conditional autonomy in 2027 and L4 in 2029, with deliveries possibly from H2 2027. It’s a testing permit only, not sales approval — and VW isn’t first (Changan and BAIC Arcfox got limited L3 product approvals in late 2025). (official VW announcement; 2027/2029 targets are company goals)

CnEVPost

Qorix gets TÜV SÜD certification for its Adaptive AUTOSAR stack (ASIL B)

Qorix has achieved TÜV SÜD certification confirming its Adaptive AUTOSAR stack complies with ISO 26262 up to ASIL B, with the certification extending to its developer tooling; it already holds ASIL D certification for its Classic AUTOSAR stack. Certified Adaptive stacks are still rare — directly relevant to anyone moving from Classic-AUTOSAR/SIL work toward the high-performance, software-defined-vehicle side. (vendor certification announcement; exact date not given in coverage)

S&P Global AutoTechInsight

BMW to bring nav-guided Level 2 city assist to Germany in 2027

Per early-November roundups, BMW plans to launch SAE Level 2 navigation-guided driver assistance for city streets in Germany in 2027 — handling turns, lane changes and roundabouts — after UNECE R171 testing. The broader read is that mainstream OEMs are re-approaching automated driving through tightly-scoped, regulation-gated L2+ features rather than big L3 leaps. (reported via industry roundups; verify timing against BMW’s own release)
