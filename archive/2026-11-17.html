Automotive & MBD

Rust keeps pushing into safety-critical ECU software

Memory-safe Rust continues to move toward ASIL-rated embedded control: AdaCore and OxidOS are reported (16 Nov) to be partnering on a Rust-based ECU operating system positioned as an alternative to — or co-resident with — heavier RTOS/AUTOSAR stacks, with OTA update-and-rollback. Both sit inside the Safety-Critical Rust Consortium (Arm, OxidOS, AdaCore, Ferrous, Woven by Toyota). Worth tracking for anyone doing AUTOSAR Classic and ISO 26262 work. (the bilateral AdaCore–OxidOS tie-up is single-source and I couldn’t open the primary; the consortium membership below is firmly attributable)

eeNews Europe

China’s ADAS market tilts toward L3 commercialisation

A new market report (16 Nov) frames “physical AI,” door-to-door (D2D) standardisation and L3 pilots as the growth drivers for Chinese independent OEMs through 2030, noting at least one Chinese OEM has cleared approval for L3 autonomous road-traffic pilots. The direction of travel — eyes-off systems reaching production — is exactly the ADAS/autonomy frontier an OEM-bound controls engineer should be watching. (vendor market report, not an OEM product announcement; forecasts to 2030)

GlobeNewswire

SDV monetisation: OTA updates forecast to top 2 billion/yr by 2035

ABI Research projects ~2.03 billion annual software-defined-vehicle OTA updates globally by 2035, and estimates true SDVs (L3 and above) at ~16.7% of new vehicles sold in 2026, as automakers shift to monetisable software platforms. For MBD/embedded engineers this is the structural pull behind centralised compute, service-oriented architecture and continuous model deployment. No major standalone ISO 26262 / Adaptive-AUTOSAR certification headline landed in the last 24–48h. (analyst forecast, dated 2 Nov; projections not actuals)
