Automotive & MBD

Hard OEM/ADAS announcements were light in the last 48 hours; the items below are the recent developments most relevant to an OEM move, dated honestly rather than padded as breaking news.

BMW and Mercedes dial back “eyes-off” Level 3, citing cost and weak demand

Both premium makers have pulled back on Level 3 hands-free/eyes-off driving in Europe through 2026, redirecting engineering toward cheaper Level 2++ assistance that competes with Tesla’s offering — BMW’s L2++ debuting on the iX3, Mercedes keeping a motorway L3 and an Abu Dhabi L4 prototype alive. For an MBD/functional-safety engineer eyeing an OEM, it’s a telling signal: the near-term money is in validated L2+ ADAS, not eyes-off autonomy.

Automotive News

China’s L3/L4 safety baseline (effective Jul 2027) sets the regulatory bar; BYD certifies ahead of the mandatory L2 standard

China’s national safety baseline for L3/L4 autonomous driving — approved 30 Jul, effective 1 Jul 2027, requiring systems to be at least as safe as a competent, attentive driver — is shaping how OEMs build their safety cases, while a mandatory L2 standard looms. BYD says every model with its “God’s Eye” system clears the new minimums and has secured safety-management certification ahead of it. Directly relevant to ISO 26262 / SOTIF safety-argument work.

CnEVPost

Mobileye to supply cloud-enhanced ADAS for future Stellantis vehicles

Mobileye will provide its cloud-enhanced ADAS (map and road-condition data fed to the vehicle to extend assisted-driving coverage) on select future Stellantis models expected from 2027 — a supplier-OEM signal of where mainstream ADAS content is heading after Stellantis shelved its in-house AutoDrive L3 programme. (announced Jul 2026; included as relevant context, not 48-hour news)

India · Cars & Bikes

Auto retail falls 23.8% YoY in October on Diwali base effect — but jumps 25% from September

FADA data shows total vehicle retails of 31,72,466 units in October, down 23.80% year-on-year (against last year’s Navratri-plus-Diwali high) but up 25.05% on September — the second-best October on record. Two-wheelers fell 28.35% YoY to 23,21,419 (up 29.67% MoM); passenger vehicles fell 14.23% YoY to 5,07,768. FADA pins the YoY drop on the high base and Diwali buying shifting into November, not collapsing demand.
