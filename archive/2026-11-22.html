AI & LLMs

Nvidia posts a record quarter — ~$115.8B revenue, data centre up ~110%, Q4 guide ~$130B

Nvidia reported about $115.8 billion in revenue for its October quarter, up roughly 103% year-on-year, with the data-centre segment alone at $107.6 billion (up ~110%). It guided to about $130 billion in Q4 data-centre revenue (±3%), and CEO Jensen Huang said Blackwell demand is “off the charts” and cloud GPUs are sold out. (these are the numbers behind the AI capex cycle — and the other side of the ~$70.7B compute bill Anthropic disclosed last week)

TechPowerUp

Google switches on Gemini “Computer Use” for the Mac

Google rolled out an agent in the Gemini app for macOS (19 Nov) that clicks, types and fills forms across local files, Mac apps and websites, running in an isolated background window with a live preview and pausing for logins, payments and other sensitive actions. It needs an Apple-silicon Mac on macOS 15+, a paid Google AI plan, and is US-only at launch. (agentic desktop control is the same direction as putting AI in the loop of real engineering tooling)

Frontier labs ship a fortnight of new models

Per release trackers, the past two weeks brought Anthropic’s Claude Fable 5.2 (9 Nov), OpenAI’s GPT-6.2 Astra and Sol (17 Nov) and Google’s Gemini 4 Neon (18 Nov), with the cheaper tiers (e.g. GPT-6.2 Sol at ~$2/$10 per million tokens) pitched at cutting the cost of complex reasoning. (tracker-sourced — model names and exact dates vary across aggregators and aren’t all confirmed on official channels)

Automotive & MBD

Wayve signs a multi-year cloud deal with CoreWeave to train its AI driver

UK autonomy firm Wayve agreed a multi-year cloud-compute deal with CoreWeave to train, evaluate and simulate its end-to-end, learning-based autonomous-driving foundation models — the data-hungry neural approach that is increasingly challenging classical, rule-based ADAS stacks. (announced mid-Nov; a quieter weekend for OEM and functional-safety news)

AutoConnectedCar

Kakao Mobility and Nvidia to co-develop a production Level-4 system

Korean ride-hailing operator Kakao Mobility signed an MoU with Nvidia to jointly develop a production-oriented SAE Level-4 autonomous-driving system, pairing its fleet data with Nvidia’s compute and DRIVE stack. (MoU stage — an intent to co-develop, not a shipping product)

AutoConnectedCar

Report: Chinese OEMs push L3 pilots, in-house chips and “cockpit-driving fusion”

A market study of China’s independent OEMs says L3 pilots are now beginning and that self-developed silicon plus merged cockpit-and-driving compute are redefining how carmakers compete, projecting continued ADAS/AD growth through 2030. (analyst market report, 16 Nov — a trend read, not a single-company announcement)

Honda Rebel 500 relaunches locally built, from ₹4.50 lakh

Honda launched a now locally-assembled (~50% localised) Rebel 500 cruiser on 19 November at ₹4.50 lakh for the standard and ₹4.90 lakh for the E-Clutch (ex-showroom, Bengaluru) — well under the ₹5.12 lakh it cost as a full import in 2025. (the engine is still imported; localisation mainly cut the on-paper price)
