AI & LLMs

OpenAI’s autonomous pipeline finds — and exploits — 948 Linux-kernel privilege-escalation bugs

In a post titled “A thousand fewer paths to root,” OpenAI describes an autonomous agent pipeline that analysed ~2,308 Linux-kernel bug reports, confirmed and built working local privilege-escalation exploits for 948 of them, and generated candidate patches — at a reported cost of about $1.2M in API compute. It is part of OpenAI’s Daybreak cyber effort; findings are being routed to kernel maintainers via its “Patch the Planet” programme rather than published as exploit code. For an embedded/MBD engineer this is the signal to watch: AI closing the full find–exploit–fix loop on exactly the kind of low-level C that runs in vehicles. (official OpenAI post; figures via its own write-up and secondary coverage)

Nvidia reportedly tells cloud partners: at least 51% of services must go to non-Chinese customers

The Financial Times reports Nvidia has told cloud-computing clients that ≥51% of their services must be sold to companies outside China, said to apply to new contracts worldwide and delivered verbally. Reuters could not verify it and Nvidia did not confirm the figure; it’s unclear whether 51% is measured by revenue, compute-hours or customers. Chinese tech stocks fell on the news (Shanghai’s STAR 50 down ~3.8%). (reported via FT; unverified, Nvidia non-committal)

Automotive & MBD

AUTOSAR ships CAPI 1.0 — executable Adaptive Platform code, not just a spec

At the 17th AUTOSAR Open Conference in Shanghai (announced 28 Oct, with partner iSOFT), AUTOSAR released CAPI 1.0: production-oriented, executable Adaptive Platform software covering all 15 core functional clusters, with source on GitHub. It marks a real shift from a specification body toward shipping reference code, with a live demo planned on an automotive ECU. AUTOSAR notes the Adaptive Platform is already in production programmes at BMW, Mercedes-Benz, VW and Xiaomi — directly relevant if you’re moving toward Adaptive and SDV work at an OEM. (official AUTOSAR; late-Oct release, most significant AUTOSAR news this cycle)

AUTOSAR

Musk: Tesla to wind down Cybertruck ~mid-2027 to free capacity for the Cybercab

In a 5 November post on X, Elon Musk said Tesla plans to wind down Cybertruck production at Gigafactory Texas around mid-2027 to free capacity for the autonomous Cybercab. Separately, Tesla’s Cybercab robotaxis have reportedly begun testing on Detroit-area roads alongside Waymo. The through-line: capacity and attention are being redirected from a niche EV toward the driverless fleet. (Musk X post; Detroit testing reported)

Yahoo Autos

Robotaxi alliances reshuffle: Waymo to end Uber exclusivity, signs Avis; Lyft leans on Baidu + Waymo

On Q3 earnings, Lyft said Waymo will end its exclusive robotaxi partnership with Uber in Austin and Atlanta by January 2028, and that Lyft is deepening ties with Baidu’s Apollo Go and with Waymo. Waymo has also signed a multi-year fleet-management partnership with Avis. The commercial map for AV deployment is being redrawn around fleet ops and platform access, not just the self-driving stack. (reported — company earnings commentary)
